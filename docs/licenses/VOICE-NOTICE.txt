Dash-Chan spoken clips: sources, tools and licences
====================================================

Scope
-----
The WAV files in assets/voice/ and the same bytes compiled into the firmware
from src/voice_data.cpp. The phrases are original Dash-Chan text, listed in
tools/voice_phrases.tsv. Dash-Chan is GPL-3.0-or-later; the clips are
distributed under the same licence as the rest of the project.

Nobody has listened to these clips by ear. They were checked only with a
speech recogniser (see "Verification"). Treat them as unreviewed until a
person has auditioned them on the device (MENU > Sound & Voice > TEST VOICE
steps through every clip).

Speech engine (build-time tool only)
------------------------------------
piper-tts 1.8.0, https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl
Licence: GPL-3.0-or-later. It embeds espeak-ng (GPL-3.0-or-later) to turn
text into phonemes.
The engine runs on a PC when tools/generate_voice.py is used. No part of the
engine, of espeak-ng or of the voice model is included in the firmware, the
flash bundle or this repository. Only the audio it produced is.

MODEL_CARD, verbatim:

    # Model card for ljspeech (high)

    * Language: en_US (English, United States)
    * Speakers: 1
    * Quality: high
    * Samplerate: 22,050Hz

    * URL: https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/
    * License: public domain

    ## Training

    See: https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/

    US English female voice. Single speaker. Trained from scratch for 1000 epochs on medium quality settings using the LJ Speech dataset. I reencoded the recordings to a bit rate of 22500 Hz so it would match other voices released for Piper TTS.

Processing chain
----------------
tools/generate_voice.py runs, for each phrase:

1. piper-tts with the model above: mono 22050 Hz 16-bit speech.
2. ffmpeg 6.1.1 (LGPL/GPL build-time tool, with librubberband and soxr):
   - silenceremove at -60 dB on both ends;
   - pitch: about five semitones up in total. Two semitones by resampling
     (asetrate 22050 * 1.12246), which also raises the formants; three
     semitones with the rubberband filter (pitch 1.18921, formant preserved),
     which also restores the engine's own speaking pace (tempo 1/1.12246);
   - highpass 180 Hz, treble +3 dB at 3.5 kHz, compressor (3:1 above -18 dB),
     limiter at 0.89, short fades at both ends;
   - soxr resample to 16000 Hz with triangular dither, mono, 8-bit unsigned
     PCM, canonical 44-byte RIFF header.
3. With --asr, a take is kept only when both recognisers named under
   "Verification" read it back word for word; otherwise the engine is run
   again, up to 12 times, and the closest take is kept and reported.

The engine adds random variation, so regenerating gives slightly different
audio. The committed WAV files are the reference.

Older recordings
----------------
spicy_pig.wav, spicy_fuzz.wav and love.wav were not made with piper. They were
synthesized for earlier Dash-Chan releases with the Windows System.Speech
(SAPI) engine through the former tools/generate_voice.ps1, using the phrases
"Pig detected! Act natural! Act natural!", "Oh man! The fuzz! Act natural!"
and "I love your face!". For this release they were trimmed and re-encoded
from 22050 Hz 16-bit to the format above (no pitch change), from the arrays
in include/voice.h as of release 0.5.0.

Verification
------------
faster-whisper 1.2.1 (MIT), models small.en and base.en, build-time only.
As committed on 2026-11-15, both read back 40 of the 44 clips word for word.
Takes were chosen with these same two models, so that number flatters the
clips; medium.en and tiny.en, which took no part in the choice, read back
38 and 30. docs/DEVELOPMENT.md lists the misses. A recogniser reading a clip
back is a sanity check; it is not a listening test, and it says nothing
about how the clip sounds on the K151 speaker.
